# OriginOrbit — clay astronaut film, logo-eye edition

## Story and visual rule

The astronaut discovers pliable clay beneath the soil on Mars, carries **that same lump** to a handmade clay space-station lab, then shapes it by hand into a flower. The station, furniture, astronaut, tools, and flower all have the same tangible stop-motion plasticine finish. No object forms itself. Each change is caused by a visible hand action.

The astronaut is an original character with a cream pitted suit and backpack, charcoal clay face, oversized ivory **OriginOrbit double-O logo shapes as eyes**, a cobalt glove on the astronaut's right hand and an orange glove on the left. The two logo eyes must stay the same shape and spacing throughout. The supplied source logo is `logo-reference.png`; `frames/character-master.png` is the character reference.

## Continuity and finishing checklist

- Reject generations that change the double-O eyes, glove sides, station porthole/hatch, or the number and color of clay lumps.
- Reject any shot where petals appear without the astronaut's hands pressing or folding the material.
- Trim duplicate held frames at the join. The shared frame should look like one continuous camera move.
- The generated eye shapes are visual approximations. For **exact logo geometry**, track and composite the original brand artwork over the eyes in the final video master.
- These concept images are 1672 × 941 px. The user's Google Flow workflow outputs 720p; a 3840 × 2160 delivery would require careful upscale and manual cleanup after generation. Upscaling changes pixel dimensions but does not make the source natively 4K. Inspect the finished moving footage before it replaces the live website film.
- Make a separate mobile composition after approving the desktop film so the character, clay and copy remain visible on a narrow screen.
